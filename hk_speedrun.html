When speedrunning a game, there are lots of different categories and even subcategories to choose from. The same goes for Hollow Knight. The following will provide the names of the most commonly ran categories and their descriptions to help you choose which one you plan on running. Let's start by explaining subcategories.

Subcategories

Subcategories, also called modifiers, are additional rules that determine what strategies are allowed while accomplishing a run. For example, while doing an Any% run, you can either play All Glitches, or No Major Glitches. But what does this even mean for Hollow Knight speedrunning?

No Major Glitches (NMG)

This is a ruleset where you cannot use certain major glitches that can dramatically skip or bypass intended parts of the game. Instead of going over every glitch in the game, let's just list the ones which AREN'T allowed:
  • Any glitch that results in invulnerability or invisibility.
  • Any glitch that allows the player to be out of bounds.
  • Any glitch using Main Menu Storage.
  • Any glitch that breaks Boss AI to where they aren't able to move or attack.
  • Wallcling storage on patches 1.3.1.5 and later.
  • Wallcling slides (where the knight achieves superslide speed using nail recoil during wallcling storage.)
  • Unclinged wallcling storage (where the knight collides with a curved surface during wallcling storage, losing the wallcling animation whle keeping the ability to continuously dash midair.)
  • Airwalk obtained from turning around on corners and seams or out of an airstall, if done on purpose or any benefit is gained from it.
  • Chaining jumps from airwalk, if done on purpose or any benefit is gained from it.
  • Seam Jumps, (A glitch introduced in the 1.5 version of the game, allowing players to jump off of the seams in vertical walls without mantis claw) if done on purpose or any benefit is gained from it.
  • Clipping underwater, or gaining normal movement while in water or acid.
  • Text storage.
  • Inventory storage, if done on purpose or any benefit is gained from it.
  • Float glitch, if done on purpose or any benefit is gained from it.
  • Dying with inventory open to regain control.
  • Quick Map Storage, focus storage, and superdash storage.
  • Unovercharm glitch
  • Charm notch duping, item duping, and room duping.
  • Duping enemy kills with Flukenest.
  • Superslides.
  • Thorn warps.
  • Hazard warps achieved by gaining early control on a room transition.
  • Televator.
  • Keeping the buff from Fury of the Fallen when not at 1HP by leaving a dream at 1HP with Dreamgate.

All Glitches

All Glitches is exactly what it sounds like. No glitches are off limits in this category. It mostly relies on Main Menu storage. Main menu storage is one of the most game-breaking glitches in the game, and because of this, it is the shortest run, with the current world record being just over 6 minutes.

What is Main Menu Storage?

Main Menu Storage is a glitch that allows for the menu to be duplicated. It can be used to have the menu open during gameplay. You can achieve this by rapidly spamming pause/escape and mouse actions on exact frames. It is used to noclip.

Elderbug% is a joke category where the player gets the Delicate Flower item and delivers it to Elderbug in Dirtmouth. Important Skips

Before going in depth, it is important to be able to know how to perform some commonly used skips. Practicing these now will help once you start learning the actual route.

Inventory Drops

This one is listed first becuase it is one of the most commonly used mechanics in speedrunning the game, and very easy to perform. On patch 1.2.2.1, opening your inventory while midair causes the Knight to fall at an increased speed. It also allows you to skip the stun that happens when falling from a high place if you close the inventory right as you hit the ground.

As you can see here, I am able to open my inventory midair and accelerate faster than the screen can even scroll to the Knight, and I regain movement right away after closing the menu.

Fireball Skips

Because spells in Hollow Knight stall you in the air, speedrunners use this to their advantage. It is typically used to cross wide gaps that would require you to have mothwing cloak first to dash across. You achieve this by jumping towards a ledge, quickly turning around, and using Vengeful Spirit. Though the exact timing and height changes depending on the location you're doing it at.

Example of a fireball skip in Greenpath. This is the first one you do in a typical Any% run. It prevents you from having to go all the way around to get to the Hornet fight, saving a lot of time.

Shade Skips

Shade Skips are skips where you die on purpose to spawn your Shade. This allows you to pogo off of it to reach heights that would be inaccessible otherwise. You can also lure enemies to do the same if you are able to, especially if they are flying enemies.

Here is an example where I use my shade to get to Salubra's shop early. I did take damage here by accident but it's important to note that sometimes taking damage on purpose is a viable strategy because of the knockback. This is called damage boosting.
